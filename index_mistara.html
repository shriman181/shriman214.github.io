The recoverable

A recoverable is a chain.

A front issues the policy and cedes the risk. A reinsurer promises to pay its share. Collateral secures that promise while the losses keep developing, long after the last policy is written. Each team holds one link: reinsurance holds the treaty, treasury the trust, the actuaries the reserves, risk the counterparty.

Each sees a fragment. None follows the recoverable.

Today, Collateral Is Checked In Pieces.

The Fragments

The reinsurance ledger records the balance. Trustees report one account at a time. Actuaries refresh reserves each quarter. Counterparty reviews come once a year. Each team sees its own slice.

None of them re-tests the collateral.

The Stale Peg

Collateral is pegged when the treaty is signed and reviewed about once a year. Long-tail casualty does not keep that calendar. Losses written ten years ago can still be developing today.

A peg set against last year's losses isn't security. It's an old estimate.

About Mistara

Mistara is software for the people who carry reinsurance credit risk: CFOs, chief risk officers, heads of ceded reinsurance, collateral and treasury managers, and reserving actuaries.

We started from one question that sits between every team on a cedent's org chart and belongs to none of them: is this recoverable secured for where the losses are going, today?

Answering it means reading four things that never talk: the paper, the losses, the collateral and the counterparty. Mistara reads all four every month, so the answer is current when it matters, before a counterparty fails rather than after.

We work alongside the systems you already run. Your reinsurance ledger keeps recording balances, your trustees keep holding assets, and your actuaries keep owning the reserves. Mistara connects them and shows where they disagree.

FAQ

Questions we get asked.

What does Mistara do?

Mistara checks that the collateral behind your reinsurance recoverables is real and still big enough. It reads your treaties, letters of credit, trust agreements, side letters and statements; confirms the collateral with the bank or trustee that holds it; re-estimates the exposure it secures from your latest loss development every month; tracks every deadline; and drafts the top-up, draw or notice for a named person on your team to sign.

Who is Mistara for?

Ceding insurers that take credit for collateralised reinsurance: fronting carriers, program and specialty carriers, and life and annuity insurers with offshore or asset-intensive treaties. The Capital edition is for the reinsurers, sidecars, ILS funds and captives that post the collateral.

Which instruments does Mistara read?

Letters of credit and their amendments, Regulation 114-style trust agreements and trustee statements, funds-withheld and modified-coinsurance arrangements, side letters, the reinsurance agreement itself, premium and claims bordereaux, and loss triangles.

What does “verified at source” mean?

We don’t take a PDF’s word for it. With your authorisation, Mistara confirms each letter of credit with the issuing or confirming bank through an authenticated channel, reconciles trustee statements line by line, prices trust assets against the trust’s eligibility schedule, and checks that each issuer is a qualified US financial institution. In 2023 about $3.36 billion of letters of credit behind reinsurance deals on one platform were alleged to be fake; a source check is how you find that out early.

How is the Cover Ratio calculated?

Verified collateral divided by the collateral your treaty would require if exposure were re-estimated from current loss development. The required amount uses your treaty’s own formula, for example a percentage of ceded case reserves, IBNR and unearned premium, after haircuts and eligibility limits. Below 1.00 means a shortfall. Every ratio shows the triangle, method and assumptions behind it, and your reserving actuary confirms the estimate.

Does Mistara send demands or draw on letters of credit automatically?

No. Mistara drafts the demand, draw presentation or notice, cites the clause and attaches the evidence. An authorised person on your team reviews it, signs it and sends it the way your treaty requires. We log receipt and funding.

Does Mistara replace our reinsurance accounting system?

No. Your ledger keeps recording balances and producing statutory reports. Mistara reads extracts from it, verifies the collateral outside it and re-tests sufficiency against development. It also prepares a support file for your Schedule F work.

Where is our data stored, and is it used to train models?

In the United States, with private deployment available for enterprise customers. Your treaties, reserves and counterparty data are never used to train models unless you opt in. We are building toward SOC 2 Type II.

What is a Collateral Sweep?

A fixed-fee review of your book, usually completed in about 30 days. We verify every letter of credit and trust at its source, re-test every program against current development, and list every shortfall with the treaty lever that closes it. It costs $40,000 to $75,000 depending on the size of the book, and the fee is credited in full against your first year’s subscription.

Does Mistara work for life and annuity reinsurance?

Yes, on the Enterprise tier. For asset-intensive treaties with funds withheld or modified coinsurance, Mistara looks through to the supporting assets and keeps the evidence your actuaries need for asset adequacy work under Actuarial Guideline 55, which took effect on 31 December 2025.

Is Mistara a law firm or an actuarial firm?

No. Mistara is software. It does not give legal or actuarial opinions. It works alongside your counsel, your appointed actuary and your reinsurance broker, and gives them a cleaner record.
